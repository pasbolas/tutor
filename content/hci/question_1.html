HCI Question 1 Study Notes

From CMPU2008 Human Computer Interaction revision material.

Q1 (a) Disciplines associated with HCI

Main idea: HCI is interdisciplinary.

Definition: Human Computer Interaction is interdisciplinary because it combines knowledge from different fields to improve how people interact with computers and digital systems.
Discipline Contribution to HCI
Computer ScienceDesigns and develops software, systems, interfaces, and interaction methods.
PsychologyExplains human behaviour, attention, learning, perception, and memory.
Cognitive ScienceStudies how people think, solve problems, and process information.
SociologyStudies how people use technology in groups, organisations, and society.
Graphic DesignImproves visual layout, colour, typography, and screen presentation.
ErgonomicsFocuses on comfort, safety, physical interaction, and reducing strain.
AnthropologyStudies human culture, habits, and behaviour in real-world contexts.
LinguisticsSupports clear communication, labels, commands, and language-based interaction.
Exam answer
HCI is interdisciplinary because it combines fields such as computer science, psychology, cognitive science, sociology, ergonomics, graphic design, anthropology, and linguistics. These fields help designers understand both the human user and the technical system.

Q1 (b) Human senses and input/output

Main idea: users provide input to the system and receive output from it.

Definition: Users interact with computers by providing input to the system and receiving output from the system. Human senses help users receive and interpret information.

Input and output

Type Meaning Examples
InputInformation sent from the user to the computer.Keyboard, mouse, touchscreen, microphone, scanner.
OutputInformation sent from the computer to the user.Monitor, speakers, vibration, printer, notifications.

Human senses in HCI

Sense Use in HCI
SightReading text, viewing icons, images, videos, colours, and layouts.
HearingAudio alerts, speech output, music, warning sounds, and voice assistants.
TouchKeyboard use, mouse use, touchscreen interaction, vibration, and haptic feedback.
SmellRarely used, but possible in specialist simulations or virtual reality systems.
TasteVery rarely used in HCI. Mostly experimental or specialist research.
Exam answer
A user may use sight to read text or view icons on a screen, hearing to receive alerts or speech output, and touch to use a keyboard, mouse, or touchscreen. Input devices allow users to send information to the computer, while output devices return information from the computer to the user.

Q1 (c) Prototypes and types of prototypes

Main idea: prototypes are early test versions of a system.

Definition: A prototype is an early version or model of a system used to test design ideas, functionality, and user interaction before final development.

Purpose of prototypes

  • Test design ideas early.
  • Collect user feedback.
  • Find usability problems.
  • Reduce development risk.
  • Improve the final system before full implementation.
Type Description Examples Advantages Limitations
Low-fidelity prototype A simple and rough version of the design. Paper sketches, basic wireframes. Cheap, quick, easy to change. Limited realism and interaction.
Medium-fidelity prototype A more detailed version with some interaction. Clickable wireframes, digital mockups. Useful for testing flow and navigation. Still incomplete and not fully realistic.
High-fidelity prototype A realistic version close to the final system. Interactive app mockups, coded interfaces. Detailed testing and realistic feedback. More expensive and time-consuming.
Exam answer
A prototype is an early model of a system used to test and evaluate design ideas. Low-fidelity prototypes are simple sketches, medium-fidelity prototypes include more detail and some interaction, and high-fidelity prototypes closely resemble the final system.

Q1 (d) Human Centred Design

Main idea: design should focus on real users throughout the process.

Definition: Human Centred Design is a design approach that focuses on users’ needs, abilities, limitations, and feedback throughout the design process.
Principle Meaning
User involvementUsers are involved during research, design, testing, and evaluation.
Understanding user needsDesign decisions are based on real user requirements.
Iterative designThe design is repeatedly tested and improved.
AccessibilityThe system should support users with different abilities and needs.
Usability focusThe system should be easy, effective, efficient, and satisfying to use.
EvaluationThe design should be tested to check whether it works for users.

Benefits

  • Improves usability.
  • Improves user satisfaction.
  • Reduces user errors.
  • Reduces training needs.
  • Creates systems that better match user requirements.
Exam answer
Human Centred Design focuses on designing systems around users’ needs, abilities, and limitations. Key principles include user involvement, understanding user needs, iterative design, accessibility, usability, and evaluation.

Q1 (e) Universal Design and Irish equality legislation

Main idea: design should support the widest possible range of users.

Definition: Universal Design means designing products, services, environments, and systems so they can be used by as many people as possible, regardless of age, size, ability, or disability.

Main factors influencing Universal Design

Factor Meaning
AccessibilitySystems should support users with disabilities, including visual, hearing, motor, and cognitive impairments.
UsabilitySystems should be easy to learn, efficient, understandable, and comfortable to use.
InclusivitySystems should consider users with different ages, abilities, backgrounds, and contexts.

Irish equality legislation

Legislation / framework Connection to Universal Design
Equal Status ActsSupport equal access and help prevent discrimination in goods and services.
Disability Act 2005Supports accessibility and participation for people with disabilities.
Public sector accessibility expectationsEncourage accessible public information, digital systems, and services.

Examples

  • Captions on videos for users with hearing impairments.
  • Screen reader support for users with visual impairments.
  • Keyboard navigation for users who cannot use a mouse.
  • Clear text and simple layouts for all users.
  • Ramps and automatic doors in physical environments.
Exam answer
Universal Design is the design of products and systems that can be used by the widest possible range of people, regardless of age, ability, or disability. In Ireland, equality and disability legislation support accessible and inclusive services. The main factors influencing Universal Design are accessibility, usability, and inclusivity.

Q1 (f) Usability

Main idea: usability measures how easy and successful a system is to use.

Definition: Usability refers to how easy, effective, efficient, and satisfying a system is to use.
Aspect Meaning
LearnabilityNew users can learn how to use the system quickly.
EfficiencyUsers can complete tasks quickly with minimal effort.
EffectivenessUsers can successfully complete their intended tasks.
MemorabilityUsers can return later and remember how to use the system.
Error prevention and recoveryThe system prevents mistakes and helps users recover from errors.
SatisfactionUsers find the system pleasant and comfortable to use.

Examples

Good usability Poor usability
Clear buttons, simple navigation, readable text, helpful error messages. Confusing menus, unclear icons, small text, hidden actions, poor feedback.
Exam answer
Usability is the measure of how easy and effective a system is to use. A usable system should be learnable, efficient, effective, memorable, tolerant of errors, and satisfying for the user.
